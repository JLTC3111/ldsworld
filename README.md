# Long D. — Project Island

A personal portfolio you can drive through. Six original projects become miniature landmarks on a quiet, low-poly island, inspired by the exploratory idea of [Bruno Simon’s portfolio](https://bruno-simon.com/).

Snow is the default weather. Three snowmen appear only while it is snowing, and two vendors stand behind detailed market stalls. A small propeller plane periodically crosses the island. Clouds have been removed completely; rain keeps short CSS streaks and overcast lighting. The roads are 2.5 times their previous width, the stream is three times wider, and every bridge has a 14.25 m wide, 10.5 m long deck with raised lantern gates and open junctions. Timber grain, metal fasteners, ropes, and glowing cage lanterns add detail. About 75% of the original trees were removed, followed by another three quarters of the remaining pines. Broadleaf trees remain. The remaining trees keep layered leaves, branches, pine sprays, and frost, with larger clearances from roads and bridge approaches. The hero title and supporting text follow the map’s sunrise, sunset, and night lighting, while the panel theme remains independent.

The playable island is 280 × 210 m. All four cars use a shared 0.63× model scale. The six project buildings now occupy the six inner plots marked in the owner's map: HR and Crypto in the north, ICUE and Contracts beside the central river, and Ceremony and Compressor in the south. Their parking, guided-tour destinations, map markers, roof labels and access lanes share the same layout data. Four cottages remain between the plots. Six narrow paved approaches connect the project parking spots to the road network. The original pale limestone roads, muted shoulders and small sage centre markings remain. Road triangles are clipped along the actual riverbanks, two-point connectors are straight, and overlapping road surfaces have separate depths to prevent flickering.

A continuous striped curb and guardrail mark the drivable island perimeter, with reflectors and physical contacts matching the shoreline limit. The same perimeter is drawn on the map. Road arrows follow the shortest road routes toward the central town; four roadside signs switch between “TOWN” and “THỊ TRẤN” with the selected language.

The Hội An Ceremony remains one of the smaller landmarks. Model size, plot foundations, rotation, tree clearances, parking, map footprints, and collision bounds all follow the same layout data. Benches and planters retain their human scale when their buildings are enlarged.

The dock’s car button opens a compact four-row chooser with thumbnails, names and a check beside the selected model. The red **8 Ball** roadster is the default; **Crocodile Monster Truck**, **Woody Pickup** and the original **Island Explorer** can be chosen with one click. The choice is remembered locally. The three supplied game-ready GLBs are copied byte-for-byte from `src/assets/3D_CARS/svg/` into `src/assets/cars/`. The runtime adapts the source axes, preserves wheel pivots and the loader’s flat-shading flag for GLBs without vertex normals, adds moving hydraulics and detailed lamps, and owns its cloned geometry and paint materials. The 8 Ball uses saturated bright red bodywork and lighter red stripe highlights; the supplied cars use clearcoat, shaped studio reflections and a restrained night visibility lift. Short-range key and rim lights follow every model, including the original Explorer, without changing the island’s day/night cycle. One small studio environment is baked per world renderer, shared across model switches and disposed with the world. The three supplied models have an ivory forward-pointing hood chevron, top-facing white front lights, red rear markers, and a wide raised rear light bar. The 8 Ball has an oversized ivory hood arrow and ivory front splitter, larger white front lamps, a wide black rear wing with upright endplates, and a thick red rear light bar; Crocodile and Woody have rear utility gates. These cues stay attached to their bodies through steering, hydraulics and flips. The original procedural variants remain as a loading-failure fallback. Selecting a car replaces its complete model, suspension, lights, steering, wheel travel, collision footprint and engine voice. Models retain matching world-space collision bounds and aerial pivots. Projector headlights, running lights, tail/brake/reverse lights, steering-linked indicators, mirrors, grilles, plates and exhausts remain functional. Reference photographs and design screenshots are excluded from production.

The expanded world includes a winding shallow stream, five drivable arched bridges, paired portals, detailed cottages and trees, glowing lanterns, rocky banks, and decorative crates, barrels, stones, cones, and metal trash cans. Six trash-can fires light automatically at night and extinguish at first light, independently of the interface theme. Only nearby fire lights are active. The water has a visible bed, animated surface normals, sky and sunlight reflections, bank foam, and car-generated wake ripples. The original Explorer uses bright coral paintwork, an ivory roof, dark trim, and a subtle night visibility lift, alloy wheels, shaped tires, panel seams, a visible interior, LED headlights, brake lamps, and an exposed hydraulic undercarriage. ## Run

Requires Node.js 20 or newer. No dependency installation or network access is required.

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Open the URL printed in the terminal (`http://127.0.0.1:4173`, or the next available port). For a production build:

```sh
npm test
npm run build
npm run preview
```

The `dist/` folder is a complete static site. Deploy it to any static host with `index.html` as the entry point. The preview server supports video range requests.
