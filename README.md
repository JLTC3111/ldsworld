# Long D. — Project Island

A personal portfolio you can drive through. Six original projects become miniature landmarks on a quiet, low-poly island, inspired by the exploratory idea of [Bruno Simon’s portfolio](https://bruno-simon.com/).

Snow is the default weather. Three snowmen appear only while it is snowing, and two vendors stand behind detailed market stalls. A small propeller plane periodically crosses the island. Clouds have been removed completely; rain keeps its slanted streaks, splashes, and overcast lighting. The roads are 2.5 times their previous width, the stream is three times wider, and every bridge has a 14.25 m wide, 10.5 m long deck with raised lantern gates and open junctions. Timber grain, metal fasteners, ropes, and glowing cage lanterns add detail. About 75% of the original trees were removed, followed by another three quarters of the remaining pines. Broadleaf trees remain. The remaining trees keep layered leaves, branches, pine sprays, and frost, with larger clearances from roads and bridge approaches. The hero title and supporting text follow the map’s sunrise, sunset, and night lighting, while the panel theme remains independent.

The playable island is back to 280 × 210 m, undoing the last fourfold expansion. Buildings and scenery keep their dimensions; all four cars are now 40% larger, with the six project landmarks spread among separate districts on the outer roads. Four cottages are scattered between them, and the buildings and trees use a more consistent scale around the larger car. An outer road loop, four connecting roads, two additional river crossings, and sparse woodland leave large open areas for future additions. The camera keeps a close driving view; snow, rain, and local shadows follow the car. The original pale limestone road finish, muted shoulders and small sage centre markings are restored; racing curbs and dark asphalt are removed. Road triangles are clipped along the actual riverbanks rather than lowered at sparse grid vertices, two-point connectors use straight curves, and overlapping road surfaces have separate depths to prevent flickering.

A continuous striped curb and guardrail mark the drivable island perimeter, with reflectors and physical contacts matching the shoreline limit. The same perimeter is drawn on the map. Road arrows follow the shortest road routes toward the central town; four roadside signs switch between “TOWN” and “THỊ TRẤN” with the selected language.

The Hội An Ceremony remains one of the smaller landmarks. Model size, plot foundations, rotation, tree clearances, parking, map footprints, and collision bounds all follow the same layout data. Benches and planters retain their human scale when their buildings are enlarged.

The dock’s car button opens a compact four-row chooser with thumbnails, names and a check beside the selected model. The red **8 Ball** roadster is the default; **Crocodile Monster Truck**, **Woody Pickup** and the original **Island Explorer** can be chosen with one click. The choice is remembered locally. The three supplied game-ready GLBs are copied byte-for-byte from `src/assets/3D_CARS/svg/` into `src/assets/cars/`. The runtime adapts the source axes, preserves wheel pivots and the loader’s flat-shading flag for GLBs without vertex normals, adds moving hydraulics and detailed lamps, and owns its cloned geometry and paint materials. The 8 Ball uses saturated bright red bodywork and lighter red stripe highlights; the supplied cars use clearcoat, shaped studio reflections and a restrained night visibility lift. Short-range key and rim lights follow every model, including the original Explorer, without changing the island’s day/night cycle. One small studio environment is baked per world renderer, shared across model switches and disposed with the world. The three supplied models have an ivory forward-pointing hood chevron, top-facing white front lights, red rear markers, and a wide raised rear light bar. The 8 Ball has a rear spoiler; Crocodile and Woody have rear utility gates. These cues stay attached to their bodies through steering, hydraulics and flips. The original procedural variants remain as a loading-failure fallback. Selecting a car replaces its complete model, suspension, lights, steering, wheel travel, collision footprint and engine voice. Models retain matching world-space collision bounds and aerial pivots. Projector headlights, running lights, tail/brake/reverse lights, steering-linked indicators, mirrors, grilles, plates and exhausts remain functional. Reference photographs and design screenshots are excluded from production.

The expanded world includes a winding shallow stream, five drivable arched bridges, paired portals, detailed cottages and trees, glowing lanterns, rocky banks, and pushable crates, barrels, stones, cones, and metal trash cans. Six trash-can fires light automatically at night and extinguish at first light, independently of the interface theme. Flames, warm flickering light, and metal cans move together when pushed. Only nearby fire lights are active. The water has a visible bed, animated surface normals, sky and sunlight reflections, bank foam, and car-generated wake ripples. The original Explorer uses bright coral paintwork, an ivory roof, dark trim, and a subtle night visibility lift, alloy wheels, shaped tires, panel seams, a visible interior, LED headlights, brake lamps, and an exposed hydraulic undercarriage. Four telescoping rams, coil springs, axles, and articulated links extend during takeoff and compress on landing. C changes the ride height.

The car cruises at 30 m/s and reaches 48 m/s with ground boost. Air steering uses quaternion orientation and angular velocity, independent momentum, nose-directed boost, and landing recovery. Space triggers one hydraulic ground jump; it has no airborne action and never opens the map. Q and E are unbound. The eastern clearing contains ten 4.64 m bowling pins that topple and slide intact when hit. They are 2.2 times their old size, with wider spacing and an 18 × 32 m lane. Pins cannot be crushed or shattered, including by blasts, and fallen pins reset upright after 18 seconds. The western clearing contains five impact-triggered TNT crates with chain reactions, debris, a brief fireball, sound, and blast impulses for the car and props. TNT labels cover all four sides and the roof. Two broadleaf trees and two wooden crates sit beside the clearing as burnable targets, away from its driving approach. A driving impact gives the car a small backward shove capped at 8 m/s with a low hop and a brief, mild time effect. Chained crates share one reaction instead of stacking momentum or airtime. Nearby loose props and trees (including leaves and winter caps) break into charred debris. Their contacts disappear with their geometry. Buildings, terrain, market stalls, bridges and portals remain intact under driving impacts and explosions. Other physical contacts map to breakable targets, including fences, signs, carts, sculpture, trees, and loose props. Strong impacts break objects; gentle contact can still push props and topple pins. Portals and their details cannot be broken by impacts or blasts, and their transport remains available. Residual flames, embers, and smoke last seven seconds; debris clears when its object respawns. Reduced motion disables the time effect and flash. R restores the complete island.

Drag any dialog by its header to move the whole window; its title also supports arrow keys, with Home to reset its position. Buttons, videos and reader scrolling retain their native interactions. ## Run

Requires Node.js 20 or newer. No dependency installation or network access is required.

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Open the URL printed in the terminal (`http://127.0.0.1:4173`, or the next available port). For a production build:

```sh
npm test
npm run build
npm run preview
```

The `dist/` folder is a complete static site. Deploy it to any static host with `index.html` as the entry point. The preview server supports video range requests.

Snow uses the snowflake artwork and five depth levels from React Bits [Pixel Snow](https://github.com/DavidHDev/react-bits/tree/main/src/content/Backgrounds/PixelSnow), adapted into the existing Three.js weather batch. It keeps the requested white/snowflake, density 0.5, speed 1, wind 0.25, flake size 1, pixel size 3, glow 0.8 and fine-pointer interaction settings. It adds no WebGL context, render target or animation loop. A 72×27 nearest-filter atlas replaces square points; a small shader halo supplies glow without postprocessing. Draw counts scale with viewport area, cap at 320 on coarse pointers and 90 with reduced motion, and reduce after sustained slow frames.
