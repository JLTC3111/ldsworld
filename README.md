# Long D. — Project Island

A personal portfolio you can drive through. Six original projects become miniature landmarks on a quiet, low-poly island, inspired by the exploratory idea of [Bruno Simon’s portfolio](https://bruno-simon.com/).

Snow is the default weather. Three snowmen appear only while it is snowing, and two vendors stand behind detailed market stalls. A small propeller plane periodically crosses the island. Clouds have been removed completely; rain keeps its slanted streaks, splashes, and overcast lighting. The roads are 2.5 times their previous width, the stream is three times wider, and every bridge has a 14.25 m wide, 10.5 m long deck with raised lantern gates and open junctions. Timber grain, metal fasteners, ropes, and glowing cage lanterns add detail. About 75% of the original trees were removed, followed by another three quarters of the remaining pines. Broadleaf trees remain. The remaining trees keep layered leaves, branches, pine sprays, and frost, with larger clearances from roads and bridge approaches. The hero title and supporting text follow the map’s sunrise, sunset, and night lighting, while the panel theme remains independent.

The playable island is back to 280 × 210 m, undoing the last fourfold expansion. Buildings and scenery keep their dimensions; all four cars are now 40% larger, with the six project landmarks spread among separate districts on the outer roads. Four cottages are scattered between them, and the buildings and trees use a more consistent scale around the larger car. An outer road loop, four connecting roads, two additional river crossings, and sparse woodland leave large open areas for future additions. The camera keeps a close driving view; snow, rain, and local shadows follow the car. The original pale limestone road finish, muted shoulders and small sage centre markings are restored; racing curbs and dark asphalt are removed. Road triangles are clipped along the actual riverbanks rather than lowered at sparse grid vertices, two-point connectors use straight curves, and overlapping road surfaces have separate depths to prevent flickering.

A continuous striped curb and guardrail mark the drivable island perimeter, with reflectors and physical contacts matching the shoreline limit. The same perimeter is drawn on the map. Road arrows follow the shortest road routes toward the central town; four roadside signs switch between “TOWN” and “THỊ TRẤN” with the selected language.

The Hội An Ceremony remains one of the smaller landmarks. Model size, plot foundations, rotation, tree clearances, parking, map footprints, and collision bounds all follow the same layout data. Benches and planters retain their human scale when their buildings are enlarged.

Four cars are available from the car button in the dock or the World panel: the red **8 Ball** roadster (the default), the green **Crocodile Monster Truck**, the cowboy-hat **Woody Pickup**, and the original **Island Explorer**. The three added cars load the supplied game-ready GLB files from `src/assets/3D_CARS/svg/`, copied byte-for-byte into `src/assets/cars/`. Their matching rendered previews appear in the garage. The runtime adapts Z-up / -X-forward axes, preserves source wheel pivots, adds working hydraulic rams and headlights, and gives the paint a restrained clearcoat and night visibility lift. The original procedural variants remain as a loading-failure fallback; the original Explorer stays available. Selecting a car replaces the complete model, suspension, lights, steering, wheel travel, collision footprint, and engine voice. The larger models retain matching world-space collision bounds and aerial pivots. The supplied cars have visible projector headlights, LED running lights, tail and brake lights, reversing lights, steering-linked amber indicators, mirrors, grilles, registration plates and exhaust outlets. Bridge lanterns and portal approaches leave room for the larger cars; portals react when the car’s bumper reaches the opening. The selected model is saved locally. Reference photographs and design screenshots are excluded from the production build.

The expanded world includes a winding shallow stream, five drivable arched bridges, paired portals, detailed cottages and trees, glowing lanterns, rocky banks, and pushable crates, barrels, stones, cones, and metal trash cans. Six trash-can fires light automatically at night and extinguish at first light, independently of the interface theme. Flames, warm flickering light, and metal cans move together when pushed. Only nearby fire lights are active. The water has a visible bed, animated surface normals, sky and sunlight reflections, bank foam, and car-generated wake ripples. The original Explorer uses bright coral paintwork, an ivory roof, dark trim, and a subtle night visibility lift, alloy wheels, shaped tires, panel seams, a visible interior, LED headlights, brake lamps, and an exposed hydraulic undercarriage. Four telescoping rams, coil springs, axles, and articulated links extend during takeoff and compress on landing. C changes the ride height.

The car cruises at 30 m/s and reaches 48 m/s with ground boost. Air steering uses quaternion orientation and angular velocity, independent momentum, nose-directed boost, and landing recovery. Space triggers one hydraulic ground jump; it has no airborne action and never opens the map. Q and E are unbound. The eastern clearing contains ten 4.64 m bowling pins that topple and slide intact when hit. They are 2.2 times their old size, with wider spacing and an 18 × 32 m lane. Pins cannot be crushed or shattered, including by blasts, and fallen pins reset upright after 18 seconds. The western clearing contains five impact-triggered TNT crates with chain reactions, debris, a brief fireball, sound, and blast impulses for the car and props. TNT labels cover all four sides and the roof. Two broadleaf trees and two wooden crates sit beside the clearing as burnable targets, away from its driving approach. A driving impact gives the car a small backward shove capped at 8 m/s with a low hop and a brief, mild time effect. Chained crates share one reaction instead of stacking momentum or airtime. Nearby loose props and trees (including leaves and winter caps) break into charred debris. Their contacts disappear with their geometry. Buildings, terrain, market stalls, bridges and portals remain intact under driving impacts and explosions. Other physical contacts map to breakable targets, including fences, signs, carts, sculpture, trees, and loose props. Strong impacts break objects; gentle contact can still push props and topple pins. Portals and their details cannot be broken by impacts or blasts, and their transport remains available. Residual flames, embers, and smoke last seven seconds; debris clears when its object respawns. Reduced motion disables the time effect and flash. R restores the complete island.

Click the bottom-right minimap or press M to open a fresh top-down render of the island, inspired by the provided `assets/aspects/map.png` reference. Twenty-nine labeled destinations are divided into exactly two views: six real project buildings, and twenty-three world objects and activities. Zoom controls show the full island or a closer area around the car. The minimap shows the whole island with live car position and heading, and stays visible while driving. Its clipped frame is 136 × 106 px on desktop and 104 × 88 px on phones. The full map uses a fresh overhead scene render with green land, sandy banks, teal water, detailed scenery, pale roads, and muted shoulders. Its daylight atlas palette is independent of world weather and lighting; capturing the atlas restores all scene and renderer state afterward. Select a marker or its destination button to teleport to a checked, clear landing spot. The full atlas scrolls vertically inside the dialog, including its lower coastline, controls and destinations. Drag any dialog by its header to move the whole window; its title also supports arrow keys, with Home to reset its position. Buttons, videos and reader scrolling retain their native interactions. The travel map and garage are available in English and Vietnamese.

Each real project building has camera-facing, extruded rooftop lettering with dark outlines, warm ivory faces, bronze depth, and a small underline. Names follow the selected language, including Vietnamese accents. Labels are part of the 3D scene and can open the project. They do not add physical obstacles.

Destroyed and fallen objects return to their authored positions automatically: cones after 12 s, pins after 18 s, crates after 20 s, TNT after 25 s, other small props after 30–50 s, sculpture after 90 s, and trees after 100 s. Fallen or displaced props also return. Due props occupying one another’s original locations restore together, preventing mutual respawn deadlocks. Respawns wait safely if the car occupies a landing area; a blocked return also postpones any props that depend on that spot. World pause and hidden tabs freeze these timers. R still restores everything immediately.

The portfolio panels use a wide, two-column studio layout, drawing inspiration from [Julian Ozen’s narrative and 3D project displays](https://www.julianozen.com/). Each project pairs an interactive physical display with its real demo, a concise case study, three supported capabilities, two annotated demo stills, and related work. Hovering or focusing a project card updates the large display; selecting a gallery still jumps to that moment in the muted video. About adds capability groups and a linked project timeline, while Contact offers conversation prompts and example projects. The left side retains the interactive 3D computer and Project/Stack information on laptop-height screens; the right side includes the selected project’s language-matched preview video. Phones stack both panels with shared scrolling. Every new section is available in English and Vietnamese.

Click the computer’s screen to switch between Project and Stack, or use the two buttons below it. The physical monitor displays project notes or the complete technology stack. The Preview tab has been removed. Select a tool below the monitor to read its role. Turning the object does not activate the screen. Keyboard users can use the same display controls and tool buttons; their readable information remains available if WebGL cannot start.

“Follow your curiosity” is the bilingual header tagline. The name and tagline use tighter vertical spacing. The location/time badge is fixed to Hanoi and current Vietnam time (UTC+7), without IP lookup. It uses an opaque theme-aware surface, with readable text in both light and dark mode. The world clock is a separate simulated day/night cycle. The header uses its own neutral charcoal/ivory palette, so the monogram and tagline stay readable over either daylight or night scenery. The name uses [React Bits Tech Text](https://reactbits.dev/text-animations/tech-text): letters reveal dashed outlines and a selection frame on pointer hover or keyboard focus. Its published canvas engine is adapted to the existing static site; a static label remains for reduced motion or unavailable canvas. Upstream attribution and the MIT + Commons Clause license are retained in `src/vendor/REACT-BITS-LICENSE.txt`.

## Run

Requires Node.js 20 or newer. No dependency installation or network access is required.

```sh
npm run dev
```

Open the URL printed in the terminal (`http://127.0.0.1:4173`, or the next available port). For a production build:

```sh
npm test
npm run build
npm run preview
```

The `dist/` folder is a complete static site. Deploy it to any static host with `index.html` as the entry point. The preview server supports video range requests.

## Explore

- WASD / arrow keys: drive on land; pitch and yaw in the air. Shift: rocket boost on land or in the air. B: brake. C: raise/lower the hydraulics.
- Click or tap the ground to drive there, with routes around buildings; click a project building to open its details. Floating project buttons and checkmark labels have been removed; buildings keep their clickable surfaces and hover cursor.
- Drag with a mouse or trackpad to orbit the camera. Right-drag or Shift-drag to pan. Scroll or pinch to zoom; Shift-scroll to pan.
- On a touchscreen, use two fingers to pan and pinch to zoom.
- Zoom allows a wider driving view up to 1.55× the default view extent, while the island remains viewport-sized. Camera tracking keeps the car clear of the header and controls.
- Space: hydraulic jump, with visible compression, piston extension, and landing rebound. One jump per landing; additional Space presses in midair have no effect. Space is intercepted even when the minimap button retains focus after its panel closes. The car recovers from awkward landings. H: honk (when sound is enabled). R: reset the car, fallen pins, TNT crates, and other props.
- While the car moves or jumps, page overlays fade while the live minimap remains visible and interactive. Stop, click the car, or press Escape to restore the controls. Projects and map shortcuts remain available; opening a panel pauses driving. Touch users can tap the car to stop.
- M or the bottom-right minimap: full top view and instant destination teleport. Car button: switch among four rides. P: projects. Escape: close a panel.
- Touch controls appear after entering the world on small screens.
- “Take a tour” drives between project stops; each detail panel leads to the next stop.
- Select EN or VI in the header or panel toolbar. Vietnamese covers the interface, project content, accessibility labels, page metadata, and world signs. Browser language is detected; `?lang=vi` or `?lang=en` also works for shared links.
- Open **World** to choose sunrise, daylight, sunset, night, or the automatic day/night cycle; select clear weather, rain, snow, or changing weather; and control the headlights. A full automatic day lasts about four and a half minutes.
- Rain and snow affect tire grip. Loose props, benches, planters, snowmen, and shoreline rocks respond to gravity, car impacts, and each other; click or tap one to give it a nudge. Snowmen participate only during snow. Trees, buildings, stalls, fences, rocks, and bridge rails use height-aware collision boundaries derived from their actual geometry, including instanced details. Fixed contacts use a spatial grid and the car’s oriented footprint, while route planning keeps a small obstacle graph. Sleeping props avoid unnecessary simulation work. R resets the car and every prop to their original positions. Streams can be crossed anywhere by driving: shallow water slows the car slightly and the suspension follows the ford bed. Bridges remain available for a dry crossing. Route planning and physical props also permit stream crossings.
- Bowling and TNT clearings are available from the map. Hit the pins to knock them down; hit TNT at speed to detonate it. Gentle contact does not trigger TNT. Chain reactions push nearby objects and give the car a short, capped backward shove. Explosions pause with the world, respect reduced motion, and use the global speaker mute.
- Drive into a glowing portal to travel to the other side of the island. Both gates also appear in the map.
- Light and dark interface themes use shared contrast-checked colors and remain independent of the world's daylight. The selected theme is remembered locally, with the system preference as the initial default.
- Project videos autoplay muted, loop inline, and pause when their panel closes or the page is hidden. Native playback controls remain available for the main demo. “Keep exploring” and Contact cards use silent video previews in the selected language; they load/play only when scrolled into view, pause offscreen, and release the old recording when the panel changes or closes.
- Switching EN/VI explicitly releases the old video resource, binds and loads the selected recording, and updates posters, monitor previews, and gallery timestamps. A visual audit found that only Ceremony and ICUE have full EN/VI pairs. Both HR, Contracts, and File Compressor sources show Vietnamese UI; both Crypto sources show English. The player labels the actual recorded language and explains a missing counterpart.
- Quiet background music loops using the local `mixkit-a-very-happy-christmas-897.mp3` soundtrack. Pause/play is immediately to the right of the speaker icon. The speaker mutes music, engine audio, and nature ambience; the music button separately remembers whether music is enabled. Autoplay is attempted on load and retries on the first trusted interaction when the browser blocks audible autoplay. Hidden tabs pause music, and explicit pause stays paused.
- Wind and flowing water use local natural-sound recordings beneath the music. Wind strengthens with rain/snow, and the stream becomes louder as the car approaches the river. Rooster calls occur only from 05:30–09:30 on the island clock, at least 32 real seconds apart. Birdsong loops during sunset, 17:00–19:00. Time presets update the soundscape immediately; rapid preset changes cannot spam animal calls. Nature ambience keeps playing quietly through panels, pauses in hidden tabs, and follows the global speaker mute. [Sound sources and licenses](src/assets/audio/AMBIENCE-SOURCES.md) document the existing wind/water assets and CC0 bird/rooster recordings.
- A quiet synthesized engine sits beneath the music and nature ambience. Its engine bus gain is more than 75% lower than the previous mix, with rounded sine fundamentals, restrained upper harmonics, lower audio-only redlines, and slower pitch/volume transitions. The four cars retain distinct low hums. Automatic exhaust pops and hard gear-change clicks are removed, with much softer intake and boost airflow. Driving speed and acceleration are unchanged. Road and grass tire noise, bridge boards, stream entry splashes, water crossings, pin clacks, wood/metal/stone impacts, landings, hydraulic jumps, portal travel, and TNT retain their separate sound layers. Audio unlocks on driving clicks or keys, retries interrupted contexts, honors mute, and stays silent at rest. H sounds the horn.
- Reduced-motion support, lighter weather effects on mobile, and the project browser are included.

Project demos always open muted. Engine audio is enabled for driving unless you mute it, and never starts before an interaction. Discovered stops are remembered only for the browser session; language, theme, and mute choice are remembered locally. There are no analytics, credentials, accounts, tracking, or backend services.

## Content and assets

Project titles, facts, links, and demo videos were taken from the original portfolio source at `full-stack-course/1.web-portfolio-en/index.html`. The external `full-stack-course/` source directory is not part of this repository.

Vietnamese project names and links were checked against `full-stack-course/1.web-portfolio-vn/index.html` in that external source directory. This retains the six selected portfolio projects without importing private application data. Videos are copied from the matching English or Vietnamese portfolio’s `public/` directory. Posters and the 20 unique annotated gallery stills are frames extracted from those videos. Original recording hashes verify source provenance; visual frame inspection verifies actual UI language. The added project stories describe demonstrated features without introducing client claims or performance metrics.

The world and showcase objects use original procedural geometry in the scene modules. Three.js r175 is vendored locally with its MIT license in `src/vendor/THREE-LICENSE.txt` so the portfolio has no CDN dependencies. The UI uses system fonts.
